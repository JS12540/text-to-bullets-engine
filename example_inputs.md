### 1. The Internet

> The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that communicate using the Internet protocol suite. Its origins can be traced to research networks developed in the United States during the 1960s, particularly ARPANET, which was funded by the Advanced Research Projects Agency. During the 1980s and 1990s, networking technologies expanded beyond academic and military institutions and became increasingly available for commercial and public use. The introduction of the World Wide Web by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989 significantly increased the accessibility of information on the Internet by allowing documents to be connected through hyperlinks. Today, the Internet supports services including email, websites, cloud computing, video streaming, social networking, electronic commerce, and online communication. Billions of people around the world use Internet-connected devices, making the network an important part of modern communication, education, business, entertainment, and scientific research.

### 2. The Solar System

> The Solar System consists of the Sun and the objects gravitationally bound to it, including eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and smaller bodies. It formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a large molecular cloud. The four planets closest to the Sun—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are terrestrial planets composed primarily of rock and metal. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are classified as ice giants. Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System and contains more mass than all the other planets combined. Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt, a region containing numerous icy bodies, including the dwarf planet Pluto. The Solar System is located in the Milky Way galaxy and takes roughly 230 million years to complete one orbit around the galactic center.

### 3. Artificial Intelligence

> Artificial intelligence is a field of computer science concerned with developing systems capable of performing tasks commonly associated with human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, perception, language understanding, and decision-making. Early research in artificial intelligence emerged as a formal academic discipline during the 1950s. Machine learning later became one of its most important subfields, allowing computer systems to identify patterns and improve performance using data rather than relying entirely on explicitly programmed rules. Deep learning uses artificial neural networks containing multiple computational layers and has contributed to advances in computer vision, speech recognition, natural language processing, and scientific research. Modern generative AI systems can produce text, images, audio, video, and computer code. Artificial intelligence is now used in industries including healthcare, finance, transportation, manufacturing, education, and entertainment, while its increasing adoption has also generated debate about reliability, employment, privacy, safety, copyright, and governance.

### 4. The Roman Empire

> The Roman Empire developed from the Roman Republic and became one of the most influential political entities of the ancient world. Augustus became Rome's first emperor in 27 BCE following decades of civil conflict. At its greatest territorial extent under Emperor Trajan in 117 CE, the empire controlled large areas of Europe, North Africa, and Western Asia. Roman authorities constructed extensive networks of roads, aqueducts, ports, and cities that supported military movement, commerce, and administration. Latin became widely used throughout the western provinces, while Greek remained important in the eastern Mediterranean. Roman law, architecture, engineering, language, and political institutions influenced many later societies. During the later imperial period, Rome experienced political instability, economic difficulties, external invasions, and divisions of imperial authority. The Western Roman Empire ended in 476 CE, while the Eastern Roman Empire, commonly known as the Byzantine Empire, continued for nearly another thousand years.

### 5. DNA

> Deoxyribonucleic acid, commonly known as DNA, is the molecule that carries genetic information in humans and most other living organisms. DNA consists of two strands arranged in a double-helix structure and is constructed from units called nucleotides. Each nucleotide contains one of four nitrogenous bases: adenine, thymine, cytosine, or guanine. The sequence of these bases encodes biological information that cells use in processes including the production of proteins. In humans, most DNA is located inside the cell nucleus, although a smaller amount is present in mitochondria. The human genome contains approximately three billion base pairs organized into chromosomes. DNA is copied during cell division so genetic information can be passed to newly formed cells. Differences in DNA sequences contribute to genetic variation among individuals, while mutations can arise through replication errors or environmental influences and may sometimes contribute to disease.
